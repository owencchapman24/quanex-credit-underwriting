# Phase 0 case charter

**Information cutoff: 2025-12-15**

**Committee date: 2025-12-15**

**Phase 0 disposition: GO to Phase 1, subject to the conditions below**

## Mandate

This is a lender-side, public-information underwriting case concerning Quanex
Building Products Corporation (NYSE: NX) after its August 1, 2024 acquisition
of Tyman plc. Quanex is a Delaware corporation and a manufacturer and component
supplier to building-products OEMs. At the cutoff it reported three segments:
Hardware Solutions, Extruded Solutions, and Custom Solutions. Its principal
end-market exposures include residential repair and remodeling, new residential
construction, and selected commercial and industrial applications.

The lender perspective is that of a participating commercial bank evaluating an
illustrative hold of up to $50 million of combined commitments. The bank is not
acting as the borrower, arranger, equity investor, or transaction advocate.

## Hypothetical request

The following are **HYPOTHETICAL PROPOSED TERMS**, not borrower-reported facts,
committed financing, or approved amounts:

| Item | Request ceiling / proposal |
|---|---:|
| Senior secured amortizing term debt | Up to $650 million |
| Working-capital revolver | $300 million |
| Participating-bank combined hold | Up to $50 million |
| Tenor | Five years |
| Projected closing | January 31, 2026 |

Pricing, original issue discount, fees, amortization, mandatory prepayments,
collateral, guarantee coverage, covenants, baskets, conditions precedent, and
allocation of the bank hold between facilities remain unspecified. Later phases
must not fill those gaps by treating the existing agreement as the proposed term
sheet.

## Central underwriting question

Do sustainable operating cash flow, accessible liquidity, and enforceable lender
protections support the requested exposure through realistic downside conditions,
and does the refinancing create enough economic and structural benefit to justify
its costs and restrictions relative to retaining the existing financing?

The permissible recommendations remain:

1. refinance as proposed;
2. refinance at a smaller amount or with a different structure;
3. refinance only with conditions;
4. retain the existing financing; or
5. decline.

## Repayment concepts

- **Primary repayment:** recurring operating cash flow after cash interest,
  taxes, working-capital needs, maintenance capital expenditure, and required
  term amortization. Acquisition synergies must be separately evidenced and
  sensitized; they are not cash flow merely because management labels them.
- **Secondary repayment:** unrestricted, accessible cash and committed revolver
  availability. In an enforcement scenario, collateral and enterprise-value
  recovery are backstops, not substitutes for demonstrated cash repayment.

## No-transaction benchmark

Retaining the existing senior secured facilities is a live alternative. As of
October 31, 2025, they matured August 1, 2029, provided a $475 million revolver,
and had $296.3 million of reported availability. The hypothetical refinancing
would therefore occur about 3.5 years before maturity and extend final maturity
by only about 18 months. A later recommendation must show a measurable benefit
after fees, required amortization, pricing, reduced revolving commitments, and
any hedge or break costs. Maturity extension by itself is not sufficient.

## Classification discipline

Every later analytical input must be identifiable as one of:

- **FACT:** supported by an allowed source in `EVIDENCE_INVENTORY.csv` and cited
  to a specific document and date.
- **ANALYST ASSUMPTION:** an explicit, sensitivity-tested judgment that is not
  represented as borrower fact.
- **HYPOTHETICAL PROPOSED TERM:** part of the case request or a later structuring
  alternative, not an existing contractual term.

The latest reported balance-sheet date available at the cutoff is October 31,
2025. No December 15, 2025 debt or cash balance is publicly determinable.

## Frozen-information protocol

1. Substantive information is eligible only if first published on or before
   December 15, 2025. Event date and publication date are distinct; the latter
   controls eligibility.
2. A later document describing an earlier period is prohibited from the original
   underwriting. It must be quarantined for a separately labeled subsequent-
   outcome review after the original decision is complete and frozen.
3. Each source must be registered with reporting/effective date, publication or
   filing date, location, intended use, and issue-specific authority.
4. Governing agreements and amendments control contractual debt terms. The 10-K
   controls audited financial-statement facts but not a more precise contract
   interpretation.
5. Search-result snippets, data aggregators, and later repostings are discovery
   aids only. They cannot support a modeled fact when the primary source exists.
6. Forecast vintages, industry data, rates, and market observations must likewise
   have publication dates at or before the cutoff.

## Evidence limitations and treatment

| Limitation | Phase 0 treatment |
|---|---|
| No public December 15, 2025 debt, cash, letter-of-credit, or revolver balance | Use October 31, 2025 only as a dated fact; closing payoff is a diligence condition. |
| No public lender compliance certificate or full covenant calculation at October 31, 2025 | Compliance is disclosed, but exact headroom is **not determinable**; reconstruct and sensitize in later phases. |
| Fee letters are not public | Existing all-in fees beyond the disclosed commitment-fee grid are **not determinable**. |
| Proposed pricing, fees, amortization, covenants, security, and guarantees are absent | Required hypothetical term-sheet inputs before a final recommendation; not a Phase 0 blocker. |
| Exact post-Tyman guarantor joinders, pledged equity, excluded assets, lien perfection, and foreign collateral are not fully public | Diligence condition and recovery-analysis limitation. |
| Interest-rate hedge positions and refinancing break costs are not sufficiently disclosed | Diligence condition; include a cost sensitivity. |
| FY2025 internal control over financial reporting retained a material weakness related to cash-flow statement preparation/review | Flag for financial diligence and model-quality controls; audited statements remain available. |
| Tyman creates comparability, integration, purchase-accounting, synergy, and segment-recast issues | Later historical work must bridge reported, pro forma, and recast periods without mixing bases. |

## Questions for later phases

1. What is sustainable EBITDA and free cash flow on a comparable FY2021-FY2025
   basis after Tyman, purchase accounting, impairments, restructuring, and
   claimed synergies?
2. How much of the $172.5 million revolver balance at October 31, 2025 is
   permanent acquisition financing versus seasonal working capital?
3. What are the borrower’s minimum seasonal liquidity needs, trapped cash,
   working-capital volatility, and foreign cash accessibility?
4. What are covenant leverage, interest coverage, and true headroom under the
   agreement definitions in base and downside cases?
5. What debt balance must actually be refinanced at January 31, 2026, and how are
   fees funded?
6. What proposed pricing, amortization, collateral, guarantee, covenant, basket,
   and mandatory-prepayment terms compensate lenders for risk?
7. What is the all-in NPV and liquidity benefit of refinancing versus retaining
   the existing facilities, including the write-off of financing costs and any
   hedge breakage?
8. What downside severities are credible for housing, repair and remodeling,
   Europe, customer concentration, tariffs/raw materials, integration, and FX?
9. What bank hold allocation produces an acceptable risk-adjusted return and
   concentration outcome?
