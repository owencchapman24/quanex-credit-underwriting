# Existing financing and refinancing logic

**Information cutoff: 2025-12-15**

**Balance date for amounts: 2025-10-31 unless stated otherwise**

## Source hierarchy

Contract terms below come from the June 12, 2024 Amendment No. 1 and its attached
conformed credit agreement, read with the original July 6, 2022 agreement. The
FY2025 10-K supplies reported balances and confirms compliance. Where the 10-K
summary conflicts with the contract, the executed agreement controls.

Revolver sublimits are $100 million for alternative currencies, $30 million for
letters of credit, and $15 million for swingline loans. These are sublimits within,

The agreement permits incremental term loans and/or revolver increases totaling
the greater of $310 million and 100% of consolidated EBITDA, in minimum $10
million increments. This is an accordion, not committed liquidity: invited
lenders may decline in their sole discretion.

## Amortization and maturity profile

The Term A amortizes in quarterly installments equal to 1.25% of original
principal, or $6.25 million per quarter / $25 million per year, with the remainder
due at maturity. The agreement permits voluntary term prepayment without premium
or penalty, subject to notice, minimum amounts, and interest-period breakage. It
also requires specified mandatory prepayments from non-permitted debt and from
certain asset-sale, insurance, and condemnation proceeds, subject to contractual
exceptions and thresholds.

The 2029 amount includes the then-outstanding revolver and Term A balloon on the
assumption that revolver usage does not change.

## Pricing and fees

Borrowings may use Base Rate or Adjusted Term SOFR (and specified alternative-
currency rate mechanics) plus a leverage-based applicable margin:

| Consolidated Net Leverage Ratio | Commitment fee | SOFR / eurocurrency / RFR margin | Base Rate margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| <= 1.50x | 0.150% | 2.00% | 1.00% |
| > 1.50x and <= 2.25x | 0.175% | 2.25% | 1.25% |
| > 2.25x and <= 3.00x | 0.200% | 2.50% | 1.50% |
| > 3.00x | 0.250% | 2.75% | 1.75% |

The FY2025 10-K reports a 6.57% rate on facility borrowings at October 31, 2025
and a 6.83% weighted-average borrowing rate for FY2025. Default interest is 2%
above the otherwise applicable rate. Undisclosed fee letters may contain other
fees; these are not determinable from public information.

## Financial covenants and the conflicting disclosure

The operative agreement has two quarterly maintenance tests:

- Consolidated Net Leverage Ratio: **not greater than 3.25x**, subject to the
  acquisition step-up described below.
- Consolidated Interest Coverage Ratio: **not less than 3.00x**.

The net leverage numerator is consolidated funded indebtedness less eligible
unrestricted cash and cash equivalents, capped at $100 million. Eligible cash is
cash of the borrower and domestic subsidiaries that satisfies accessibility and
lien conditions; cash in non-U.S. accounts is capped at $25 million for this
definition. Denominator construction is contractual consolidated EBITDA, not the
company's press-release Net Debt / LTM Adjusted EBITDA metric.

### Preserved conflict

The FY2025 10-K contains two opposite formulations:

- MD&A says Quanex “must not permit ... [the ratio] to be greater than 3.25 to
  1.00.”
- Note 9 says the ratio “must be greater than 3.25 to 1.00.”

Section 7.1 of the conformed governing agreement says Quanex “shall not permit
... [the ratio] to be greater than 3.25 to 1.00.” The agreement therefore makes
3.25x a maximum. The Note 9 “must be greater” wording is preserved as a source
error and is not silently corrected.

For a qualifying acquisition over $100 million, the agreement permits the
borrower, on notice, to increase the maximum to 3.75x for the specified
four-quarter period. Quanex's June 5, 2025 release stated that the lender covenant
limit was below 3.75x through fiscal Q4 2025 and below 3.25x starting fiscal Q1
2026. On that evidence, the October 31, 2025 test used the acquisition step-up;
the standard 3.25x maximum applies to the January 31, 2026 quarter-end test,
absent another permitted step-up. Quanex disclosed compliance at October 31,
2025 but did not publish the full calculation or numerical headroom.

## Guarantees, collateral, and restrictions

The facilities are guaranteed by the borrower's subsidiaries other than excluded
subsidiaries. Excluded subsidiaries include foreign subsidiaries and qualifying
immaterial domestic subsidiaries; the latter are subject to individual 2.5% and
aggregate 5% asset/revenue tests in the agreement. New material domestic
subsidiaries generally must join the guaranty and security agreement.

The obligations are secured by first-priority liens, subject to permitted liens
and excluded assets, on substantially all assets of the loan parties. The 10-K
describes substantially all domestic assets other than real property as pledged.
The agreement does not require a foreign-subsidiary guaranty; it contemplates up
to 65% of voting and 100% of nonvoting equity interests of a first-tier foreign
subsidiary where applicable. Exact post-Tyman joinders, pledged equity, excluded
assets, and perfection are not fully determinable publicly and require diligence.

Material negative covenants restrict, subject to baskets and exceptions,
additional debt and liens, fundamental changes, asset dispositions, junior-debt
prepayments, restricted payments/dividends, investments, affiliate transactions,
and negative pledges/restrictive agreements. The 10-K summary cites a $35 million
annual dividend limit, while broader restricted payments are governed by detailed
baskets and conditions in the agreement. Certain actions require pro forma net
leverage no greater than 2.75x and liquidity above $25 million.

## Commercial logic of the hypothetical refinancing

There is a plausible reason to test the transaction, but no demonstrated economic
case yet.

**Potential benefits:**

- The revolver remained materially drawn at both October 31, 2024 ($222.5m) and
  October 31, 2025 ($172.5m). Moving acquisition-related permanent usage into
  amortizing term debt could restore the revolver to a working-capital role.
- On October 31 balances, $650 million of term proceeds could repay $641.25
  million of facility principal, leaving only $8.75 million before fees and other
  uses. This is an illustration, not a closing forecast.
- If all current facility principal moved into the term loan and $6.2 million of
  LCs rolled, a $300 million revolver would initially provide about $293.8 million
  of availability—approximately the existing reported $296.3 million—despite the
  lower commitment.
- A January 31, 2031 maturity would extend the existing August 1, 2029 maturity by
  about 18 months and defer a concentrated 2029 refinancing requirement.

**Potential disadvantages / reasons to retain existing financing:**

- The existing facilities still had about 3.5 years to maturity at the proposed
  closing; there is no near-term maturity wall compelling an early refinance.
- The proposed revolver is $175 million smaller. Similar day-one availability
  depends on the term loan taking out all revolver usage and says nothing about
  peak seasonal needs or downside draws.
- Proposed pricing, amortization, fees, covenants, baskets, and prepayment terms
  are unknown, so interest savings and flexibility cannot be claimed.
- Quanex had $11.0 million of unamortized financing fees at October 31, 2025;
  accounting write-off, new issuance costs, and hedge/break costs could erode or
  eliminate the extension benefit.
- A $650 million term loan could increase mandatory amortization or overfund the
  actual payoff if debt declines before closing.
- Existing collateral and covenant protections are already substantial; a new
  package could be more restrictive without sufficient pricing or liquidity gain.

The later model must compare refinance and no-transaction cases on the same cash
flow, rate, covenant-definition, and downside assumptions. The transaction should
not proceed merely because the case request supplies a headline amount.
